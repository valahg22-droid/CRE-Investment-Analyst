Project 1: Medical Office Lease Analysis (Clermont, FL)

Context. A chiropractic practice was negotiating a lease for a small medical office suite in Clermont. My team represented the tenant, the model is built from the terms in the Letter of Intent (LOI).

Why the model was built. To help the tenant understand the full cost of the lease beyond the quoted rent, and to show which terms were worth negotiating. It turns the LOI into a monthly cash flow covering the initial term and both renewal options. It then compares the deal against possible landlord counteroffers and tenant-friendly alternatives.

What it covers. Monthly rent schedule, NNN charges, free rent, effective rent, NPV of lease payments, rent as a share of practice revenue, and sensitivity tables for the main negotiation terms.

Project 2: Warehouse Acquisition (Ormond Beach, FL)

Context. A client planned to make an offer on a small industrial warehouse in Ormond Beach, to be leased to a tenant on a triple-net (NNN) basis. The client supplied the offer price, down payment, and lease terms. The rest of the inputs were assumptions.

Why the model was built. To show the client what his proposed offer could look like as an investment before he submitted it. The model projects income and returns over 5, 8 and 10-year holds. It compares his down payment against a standard 70% loan-to-value loan and checks whether the property's income can comfortably cover the loan payments.

What it covers. Lease rent schedule, multi-year pro forma, loan structure, debt coverage (DSCR), sale at exit, IRR and equity multiple, plus an investment memo with a recommendation.

Modeling Approach
All inputs are kept on one tab, separate from the calculations
Color coding: blue = input, black = formula, yellow fill = assumption to confirm
Results are checked against the source document (for example, the lease model matches the LOI's Year 1 rent)
Sensitivity and scenario tables cover the variables that drive each decision
