# Commercial Real Estate Financial Modeling Portfolio

This portfolio showcases my commercial real estate (CRE) financial modeling skills in Excel through three applied analysis scenarios. Each model is organized to make its assumptions, calculations, outputs, and decision-useful conclusions easy to follow.

The examples demonstrate how I structure a real estate question, translate deal terms and property assumptions into a financial model, and present the results to support an investment or leasing decision.

> **Portfolio note:** These scenarios were created for demonstration purposes for client prospecting. Some information I used in the models is hypothetical. The information in these models is not investment, legal, tax, or valuation advice.

## Scenario 1: Medical Office NNN Lease Analysis

**Perspective:** Tenant representative

**Purpose:** Evaluate the proposed lease economics for a medical office in Clermont, Florida using the transaction’s Letter of Intent (LOI) as the source of business terms.

This analysis translates the LOI into a tenant-focused Excel model to compare the proposed rent and lease structure over the contemplated term. It can be used to review base rent, escalations, lease timing, tenant improvement and other concessions, and estimated occupancy costs, including NNN expenses where provided. The model is intended to help the tenant understand the financial implications of the proposed terms and identify items for review or negotiation.

**Excel skills demonstrated:** LOI term abstraction, rent schedule construction, lease cash flow analysis, NNN expense treatment, concession analysis, and scenario comparison.

**Files:**

- `01-medical-office-nnn-lease/overview.pdf` — scenario background, assumptions, and key findings
- `lease-analysis medical Office.xlsx` — tenant-side lease analysis model

## Scenario 2: Warehouse Acquisition and NNN Hold Analysis

**Perspective:** Owner/investor

**Purpose:** Assess the acquisition of an industrial warehouse in Ormond Beach, FL intended to be leased to a tenant under an NNN lease and held for a defined investment period.

The model connects acquisition costs and financing assumptions with lease-up, rental income, operating expenses, and the planned hold period. It can project property cash flows, debt service, investor cash flows, and sale proceeds at disposition. Key outputs may include net operating income (NOI), yield measures, equity returns, and sensitivity to changes in rent, vacancy, expenses, financing, and exit assumptions. The analysis was created at the buyer's request to understand the opportunity, and it frames the acquisition as an investment decision rather than treating the NNN lease label alone as proof that the property has no owner costs or risks.

**Excel skills demonstrated:** Acquisition underwriting, lease and rent modeling, NNN expense assumptions, debt and equity cash flows, hold-period analysis, exit valuation, Rent Schedule creation.

**Files:**

- `02-warehouse-acquisition-nnn-hold/overview.pdf` — scenario background, assumptions, and key findings
- `02-warehouse-acquisition-nnn-hold/acquisition-model.xlsx` — warehouse acquisition and hold model

## Scenario 3: Vacant Land Development Analysis

**Perspective:** Developer/Owner

**Purpose:** Created to analyze, at a high level, a vacant land parcel’s potential development and whether it appears financially feasible under stated assumptions. This vacant Land lot is located in Kissimmee, Florida.

The model organizes land and development assumptions into a project-level feasibility analysis. Depending on the scenario, this may include site and building area, program and unit mix, timing, hard and soft costs, financing, lease-up or sales assumptions, and contingency. It compares projected project costs and revenues over time and highlights the assumptions that most influence feasibility. Any zoning, permitting, environmental, engineering, market, or construction assumptions are identified as inputs requiring independent verification.

**Excel skills demonstrated:** Development Analysis, area and program calculations, project timing, construction-period cash flow, financing assumptions.

**Files:**

- `03-vacant-land-development-feasibility/overview.pdf` — scenario background, assumptions, and key findings
- `03-vacant-land-development-feasibility/feasibility-model.xlsx` — land development feasibility model

## Modeling Approach

Across the three scenarios, the work is designed to show how I:

- Build transparent, assumption-driven Excel models with clear inputs, calculations, and outputs.
- Translate transaction terms or development assumptions into cash flows.
- Make timing, costs, revenues, and financing assumptions visible and traceable.
- Use sensitivities or scenarios to show how changes in key assumptions affect results.
- Summarize model outputs in plain language to support a decision.

## How to Review the Files

Start with each scenario’s overview PDF for the question, perspective, assumptions, and takeaways. Then open the corresponding Excel workbook to review the model structure and calculations. Workbook outputs depend on the assumptions shown in each file; they should not be interpreted as forecasts or conclusions about an actual property or transaction.
